# Hoverboards Guide — competitor and reference research (11 October 2026)

Method: web search summaries only. Competitor pages could not be fetched directly from this environment, so layouts were judged from search descriptions, titles and the snippets quoted below. Nothing was copied; the v3 theme is an original implementation.

## Patterns adopted (in our own way)

- **Decide-by-rider navigation**: who (kids / adults), where (all-terrain), how much (budget), extras (Bluetooth) and safety/troubleshooting as first-class destinations.
- **One comparison table** with the fields buyers actually compare: wheels, max rider weight, top speed, range, certification, Bluetooth, review link, Amazon link; filters by rider; sortable.
- **Finder** instead of a quiz that "picks the best": four questions → guides to read first plus the matching pick, with a caveat about age, weight limits and protective gear.
- **Spec honesty**: every figure is labelled advertised or verified, with a date; where our own articles disagree (e.g. Gyroor Warrior 220 lb vs 265 lb, Hover-1 Dream 160 lb vs 220 lb) the product entry says so.
- **UL 2272 guidance** follows the CPSC: certification covers the whole board, look for the mark on the packaging and the underside, a UL-listed battery or charger alone is not enough, and a price far below similar boards is a warning sign.
