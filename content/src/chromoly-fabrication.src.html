You've measured the car, sketched the hoops and maybe even bought a bender. Then the big question lands: DOM vs 4130 chromoly roll cage tube. Which one is stronger? Which one is lighter? And which one will your tech inspector actually accept?

It's one of the most argued topics in garage fabrication. Forums are full of strong opinions and numbers with no source. This guide cuts through it. You'll learn what DOM and 4130 really are, how they compare on paper, and how each one behaves in the bender and under the torch. Most of all, you'll learn why your rulebook makes this choice before you do.

One thing up front. Benders Hub is not a tech inspector or an engineer. A roll cage protects a life, so treat everything here as background reading. Confirm every detail with your current rulebook and your inspector before you buy a single foot of tube.

⚡ Quick answer

Both can make a strong, legal cage. Your rulebook, your welding skills and your bender decide which one fits.

  • DOM mild steel is the forgiving choice. It springs back less, and it can be MIG or TIG welded where the rules allow. It's the usual first-cage tube. See how to choose.
  • 4130 chromoly saves weight mainly when your rules allow a thinner wall. It springs back about twice as much as mild steel and needs a careful TIG process. NHRA rulebooks have required TIG for 4130.
  • Check first: read what the rulebooks say about material, size, wall, bend radius and welding.

What DOM and 4130 chromoly actually are

Let's start with plain definitions. The names sound technical, but the ideas are simple. Here's the catch that confuses most people: one name describes how a tube is made. The other describes what the steel is made of.

DOM: a way of making tube

DOM stands for drawn over mandrel. Speedy Metals describes the process like this. Mild steel strip (they use C1020) is formed into a tube and electric-resistance welded. The tube is annealed, then cold drawn through a die and over a mandrel. That drawing step makes the weld line almost invisible and evens out the wall. Speedy Metals says DOM generally conforms to ASTM A513 Type 5 (Speedy Metals).

So DOM starts life as welded tube. The extra drawing step is what sets it apart from plain welded tube. Ryerson lists high strength, a good surface finish and excellent concentricity among DOM's benefits (Ryerson).

4130 chromoly: a type of steel

4130 is an alloy steel. "Chromoly" is short for chromium-molybdenum, its two key alloying elements. Cage-grade 4130 is commonly sold as seamless tube in the normalized condition, often marked "N". Aircraft Spruce, for example, says all of its 4130 tubing is condition N (Aircraft Spruce). Normalizing is a heat treatment that evens out the steel's grain.

CDS and ERW: the other names you'll see

CDS means cold drawn seamless. It's made by piercing a solid bar, so there's no weld seam at all. Industrial Tube and Steel notes that CDS is normally made from SAE 1018 mild steel (Industrial Tube and Steel). ERW is welded tube without the drawing step. Some rulebooks, such as SCCA Time Trials, ban ERW for cages, as you'll see below. Want more terms explained? Our glossary covers them.

Notice something surprising? On these supplier pages, the typical yield figure for DOM is actually higher than the normalized 4130 figure. Yield is the stress where steel starts to bend for good. Tensile strength is where it finally breaks. 4130's listed edge is in tensile strength, not yield.

Also note the suppliers don't agree with each other. Online Metals lists 97.2 ksi tensile for normalized 4130. Aircraft Spruce lists 90,000 psi for its seamless 4130 tube. Neither is wrong. Mills, sizes and lots vary. That's exactly why you ask for the cert.

4130 isn't magic steel. It's different steel. Its real advantage in a cage usually comes from the rulebook letting you use a thinner wall.

What the rulebooks say about DOM and 4130

This is the section that matters most. If you race, or ever plan to, the sanctioning body decides your tube. It sets the material, the OD, the wall, the bend radius and often the welding method. Here's what we could confirm on each body's own site, and where to read the rest.

🛑Read the current edition, then ask your inspector

Rules change every year, and classes have their own supplements. Benders Hub is not a tech inspector or an engineer. Before you buy tube, read the current rulebook for your class and confirm your plan with your tech inspector.

NHRA

NHRA's General Regulations (the 2017 and 2019 editions posted on NHRA's site) say all 4130 chromoly tube welding must be done by an approved TIG (heliarc) process. Mild steel may be welded by approved MIG or TIG. Those editions also call for welds free of slag and porosity. NHRA says the digital 2026 Rulebook includes amendments issued through July 14, 2026, and its 2026–2027 amendments take effect January 1, 2027. Find both under NHRA Racer rules.

SCCA Time Trials

SCCA's Time Trials full safety rules list seamless or DOM mild steel (SAE 1020 or 1025 recommended), alloy steel (SAE 4130 or T45) or Docol R8. Alloy and mild steel may not be mixed, and ERW is not allowed. Bend radius at the tube centerline must be at least three times the tube diameter. Alloy steel welding must follow accepted industry practice. Tube size scales with vehicle weight.

FIA, NASA and SFI

FIA Appendix J, Article 253, specifies cold-drawn seamless carbon steel with a minimum tensile strength of 350 N/mm² in its cage rules. The 2026 text is dated 10 December 2025 (FIA Article 253). NASA publishes its Club Codes and Regulations on its rules page. SFI publishes drag-racing chassis specs on its chassis page.

Strength and weight: is chromoly really lighter?

Here's the honest answer: only if the wall is thinner. DOM and 4130 are both steel. So a foot of 1.75" × .120" 4130 weighs about the same as a foot of 1.75" × .120" DOM. The weight saving comes from using less steel, and that means a thinner wall.

The simple math

The steel in a tube's cross-section is roughly π × (OD − wall) × wall. Let's compare two sizes. These come from one entry in NHRA's 2019 General Regulations: 1-3/4" × .118" wall for mild steel, or .083" for 4130. They're an old example, not your rule.

  • 1.75" × .118": π × 1.632 × .118 ≈ 0.605 square inches of steel.
  • 1.75" × .083": π × 1.667 × .083 ≈ 0.435 square inches of steel.
  • The difference: about 28% less steel per foot for the thinner wall.

Multiply that across every tube in a full cage and the saving gets real. That's why drag racers and serious road racers chase chromoly. But if your rulebook sets the same wall for both materials, 4130 saves almost nothing in weight. You'd just pay more and work harder to weld it.

Strength isn't just one number

A cage has to bend and absorb energy in a crash, not just resist breaking. Wall thickness, tube diameter, the layout, the joints and the weld quality all matter. A thin-wall chromoly cage with poor welds can be weaker than a well-built DOM cage. The rulebook's table is the sanctioning body's answer to "how strong is strong enough." Don't try to out-engineer it with a supplier's spec sheet.

Bending 4130 vs DOM: springback, capacity and radius

Chromoly and DOM bend on the same rotary-draw bender with the same dies. But 4130 behaves differently, and it asks more of your machine. Plan for three differences.

1. Springback roughly doubles

JD2's Model 32 manual says 4130 springs back about twice as much as the same size and wall of mild steel (JD2 manual). For scale, Rogue Fab shows a mild-steel bend that read 86.5° after release from a 90° target. Your chromoly number will be different, and likely bigger. Pro-Tools suggests logging the heat or batch number from the mill cert, because springback varies by batch (Pro-Tools). Our springback guide walks through the test-bend method.

2. Bender capacity drops

Makers publish lower limits for 4130 than for mild steel. Check the chart for your bender before you order tube.

3. Radius rules still apply

SCCA Time Trials sets a minimum centerline radius of three times the tube diameter. Pro-Tools puts the practical non-mandrel minimum around 3 × OD, and Baileigh suggests 4 × OD as a starting point. Baileigh also notes popular off-road dies of 1.5" and 1.75" at about 5.5" and 6.5" CLR. See our roll cage bend radius guide.

⚠️No bottle-jack benders for cage tube

Ram-style "pipe benders" push the tube instead of drawing it around a die. Harbor Freight's own 12-ton manual says its bender isn't meant for thin-wall pipe. Never use one for DOM or chromoly cage tube.

Welding DOM and 4130: TIG, MIG and the rules

Welding is where the two tubes really part ways. DOM is mild steel, and it's forgiving. 4130 is an alloy steel, and it rewards a careful, consistent process. Start with the rules, then follow a proper procedure.

What the rules say

As covered above, NHRA's 2017 and 2019 General Regulations require 4130 to be TIG (heliarc) welded, while mild steel may be MIG or TIG. SCCA Time Trials doesn't name a process in what we saw. It says alloy steel welding must follow accepted industry practice, and it recommends (but doesn't require) a certified AWS D1.1 welder. Always check the current edition for your class.

Preheat: the sources disagree

Lincoln Electric says thin-wall tubing under .120" wall doesn't typically need the normal 300°F to 400°F preheat to get acceptable results. It still wants the tube at about 70°F or warmer (Lincoln Electric). ESAB's guide, though, lists 400°F to 500°F for AISI 4130 (ESAB). Those don't match. Follow a qualified welding procedure for your exact tube and wall, not a forum post.

Filler and stress relief

Lincoln calls ER70S-2 filler an acceptable alternative to ER80S-D2 for thin-wall chromoly. It notes the weld will typically undermatch the 4130's strength, and good joint design such as gussets can make up for it. Lincoln also says thin-wall tubing normally doesn't need stress relief. ESAB warns that high heat input in thin sections can cause distortion or burn-through.

💡Can't TIG yet? Plan the hand-off

Many builders bend, notch and tack the cage themselves, then hand final welding to a qualified welder. Ask your welder and inspector what they need to see first.

Cost, sourcing and mill certs

Material choice isn't just about strength. It's also about what you can buy, what you can afford and how long the build will take. Here's how the two compare off the bender.

Cost

4130 tube usually costs more per foot than DOM. TIG welding is also slower than MIG. So a chromoly cage tends to cost more in both money and hours. That's fine if the rules give you a lighter cage in return. It's wasted if they don't.

Availability

Before you design around a size, call your supplier. Ask whether they stock your exact OD and wall in the material you need. Tube that's easy to find in one size can be scarce in another. Don't assume a forum build's tube is on your local rack.

Mill certs and labels

A mill test report (often called a mill cert) lists the material, specification and test results for a batch of tube. Ask for one with every order. Inspectors may ask what your cage is made from, and the cert is your proof. It also gives you the heat number, which helps you track springback by batch.

Label every stick as it comes in. Write the material, OD and wall on each end with a paint marker. DOM and 4130 look almost the same once they're cut. SCCA Time Trials doesn't allow alloy and mild steel tube to be mixed in the cage. A mixed-up offcut could cause a problem at tech.

  • ✅ Get a quote for both materials in your exact sizes
  • ✅ Ask for mill certs before the tube ships
  • ✅ Store chromoly and DOM on separate racks
  • ✅ Keep offcuts labelled for test bends and weld coupons

Four questions to ask yourself

  1. What does my rulebook allow? List the materials, sizes and walls for your class and weight.
  2. Can I meet the welding rule? If 4130 needs TIG and you can't TIG to a high standard, plan a pro welder or choose DOM.
  3. Can my bender handle it? Find the maker's 4130 rating. No rating means no promise.
  4. Is the weight saving real? Compare the walls. Same wall, little gain.

Real-life lessons from cage builders

These are composite stories. The same situations come up again and again in garage-fab forums and build threads. Each one has a lesson that's cheaper to learn here than in your garage.

💬 The chromoly cage nobody could weld

A builder orders 4130 because "it's stronger." The bends go fine. Then they read the drag-racing rulebook for their class and see the TIG requirement for 4130. They only own a MIG. The cage sits tacked for months until they find a TIG welder to finish it.

Lesson: read the welding rule before you choose the material, not after.

💬 Same wall, no savings

Someone pays extra for chromoly to save weight in a track car. Later they notice their rulebook table lists the same minimum wall for both materials in their weight class. The cage weighs about what DOM would have.

Lesson: chromoly's weight saving comes from a thinner wall. If the rules don't allow one, DOM may be the smarter buy.

💬 The short main hoop

A builder dials in springback on DOM scrap, then bends the main hoop in 4130 using the same numbers. Both bends come out short, and the hoop won't sit on the floor plates. JD2 says 4130 springs back about twice as much as mild steel.

Lesson: test-bend the actual material, and ideally the same batch, before the first real bend.

Common DOM and chromoly mistakes (and how to fix them)

Most material regrets come from a short list of mistakes. Here they are, with the fix for each.

❌ Buying tube before reading the rulebook. The material, size or wall turns out to be wrong for your class.

✅ Fix: read the current edition first, then confirm your plan with your tech inspector. Buy tube last.

❌ Assuming chromoly is always stronger and lighter. At the same wall, it weighs about the same as DOM.

✅ Fix: compare the rulebook's walls for each material. Only a thinner wall brings a real weight saving.

❌ MIG welding 4130 where the rules call for TIG. NHRA rulebooks have required TIG for 4130.

✅ Fix: check the welding rule for your class. If TIG is required and you can't do it well, hire a qualified welder.

❌ Mixing DOM and 4130 in one cage. SCCA Time Trials doesn't allow alloy and mild steel to be mixed.

✅ Fix: pick one material for the cage, label every stick, and store them apart.

❌ Bending 4130 beyond the bender's rating. Many makers list a lower limit for chromoly.

✅ Fix: find the published 4130 rating for your bender. If there isn't one, ask the maker before you try.

❌ Reusing mild-steel springback numbers. 4130 springs back about twice as much.

✅ Fix: make a test bend in the actual tube, measure it and log it with the heat number.

❌ Trusting forum numbers as rules. Sizes quoted online are often from old editions or other classes.

✅ Fix: only build to numbers you've read in the current official rulebook for your class.

Pro tips for working with chromoly and DOM

These habits come up again and again among builders whose cages pass inspection the first time. None of them costs much. All of them save tube, time and stress.

  • 💡 Buy a test stick of the real material. Use it for springback tests and weld coupons. DOM scrap won't tell you how 4130 behaves.
  • 💡 Log the heat number. Write it next to each springback reading. A new batch can behave differently.
  • 💡 Talk to your inspector early. Show your sketch, material and sizes before you buy tube. A five-minute chat can save a cage.
  • 💡 Notch tight. Good fit-up matters even more with thin-wall chromoly. Gaps make welding harder. Our tube notching guide covers the method.
  • 💡 Clean before you weld. Remove mill scale, oil and marker from the joint area. Clean metal makes the welder's job easier.
  • 💡 Practice on coupons. Weld scrap joints in the same tube, then cut them open or have them checked.
  • 💡 Plan the whole build first. Our roll cage build guide walks through planning, bending, notching and fit-up.
🎯Expert habit: one material per car

Experienced builders pick one cage material per car and stick to it, from main hoop to the last gusset. It keeps welding simple, avoids rule problems and makes your springback notes reliable.

One more habit: keep your own safety in mind while you work. Chromoly and DOM both throw sharp burrs when cut, and welding fumes need good ventilation. Read our workshop safety notes before a long cage session.

Frequently asked questions

Is 4130 chromoly stronger than DOM?

Not across the board. Supplier figures show higher tensile strength for normalized 4130, but one supplier's typical yield figure for 1020 DOM is higher than another's for normalized 4130. Its real cage advantage is that some rulebooks allow a thinner wall.

Is a chromoly roll cage lighter than DOM?

Only if the wall is thinner. Both are steel, so the same OD and wall weigh about the same. Check whether your rulebook lists a thinner wall for 4130.

Can you MIG weld a 4130 roll cage?

It depends on your rulebook. NHRA's 2017 and 2019 General Regulations require TIG for 4130 tube. Check the current edition for your class, and follow a qualified welding procedure.

Is DOM legal for roll cages?

Many rulebooks list it. SCCA Time Trials, for example, allows seamless or DOM mild steel, but not ERW. FIA specifies cold-drawn seamless carbon steel. Always check your series.

Does chromoly need a different bender?

Not a different type, but often a bigger one. Makers usually publish a lower limit for 4130 than for mild steel, and 4130 springs back about twice as much.

Your tube-buying checklist

Before you order a single stick, run through this list. If you can tick every box, you're ready to buy.

  • I've read the current rulebook for my class, including any supplement
  • I know the allowed materials, OD and wall for my car's weight
  • I know the minimum bend radius and my die meets it
  • I know which welding process the rules allow for my material
  • I can weld that process well, or I've booked a qualified welder
  • My bender has a published rating for my material, OD and wall
  • I've confirmed my plan with my tech inspector before buying tube
  • I've asked the supplier for mill certs with my order
  • I've planned one material for the whole cage
  • I've bought extra tube for test bends and weld coupons

DOM or chromoly, the best cage tube is the one your rulebook allows, your bender can handle and your welder can join properly. Get those three right and the rest is careful, patient work. 🔧
