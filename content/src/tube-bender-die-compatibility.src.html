You've found a great deal on a used die. Or a friend has a box of dies from his old bender. Or you're wondering if your new bender can take dies from a cheaper brand. It's one of the most common questions in garage fab: are tube bender dies interchangeable? This guide to tube bender die compatibility sticks to what makers and retailers actually state, and says "unknown" everywhere else.

Dies are where the real money goes. The bender frame is a one-time purchase, but every tube size and every bend radius needs its own die set. So buying the wrong die isn't a small mistake. It can mean a die that won't seat, a pin that won't go through, or a bend that comes out wrong.

Here's what you'll get: a compatibility matrix for popular benders from JD2, Rogue Fab, Baileigh, Pro-Tools, Woodward Fab, Eastwood and Harbor Freight. You'll learn which parts of a die set have to match, how pin size and machine version change things, and a simple way to check fit before you spend a cent. Where nobody has published an answer, we'll tell you to ask the maker.

⚡ Quick answer

Assume tube bender dies are not interchangeable between brands unless the maker or retailer says so in writing.

  • Some dies swap within a brand: Rogue Fab says its M601, M605 and M625 share dies. Woodward Fab says its dies fit the WFB2 and WFPB1000. JD2 says Model 3 dies fit the Model 4.
  • Some don't, even within a brand: Baileigh's drop-on die sets are marked "Not RDB-050". JD2 says no other current models interchange.
  • Cross-brand fit: no maker we checked states it. The honest answer is unknown, ask the maker. See the matrix.

Why tube bender dies rarely swap between brands

A rotary-draw die looks simple. It's a round, grooved wheel with a clamp and a follower. But it has to match the bender in several hidden ways. The pin hole has to fit the bender's main pin. The die's height has to line up with the frame. The clamp and the follower block (some makers call it a counter die or pressure die) have to sit in the right place. And the drive features have to lock the die to whatever turns it.

Every maker designs those details for its own frames. There's no shared industry standard for garage benders that we've seen any maker claim. So even two dies with the same tube size and the same centerline radius (CLR) can be completely different underneath.

Pin size is a good example. JD2 says its Model 32 uses large 1-1/4" alloy pins, compared with 7/8" pins on its Model 3 (JD2). That's two benders from the same maker, with different pins. Rogue Fab says its benders built from 2023 use a 7/8" main pin, while older ones may use 3/4" (Rogue Fab FAQ).

⚠️"Looks close" isn't a fit

A die that almost fits can slip, shift or crack under load. That ruins tube at best, and it's a safety risk at worst. Only trust a fit statement from the maker or the retailer selling the die.

That's why this guide has one firm rule. If a maker or retailer hasn't stated a fit, we don't claim it. Forum posts and Q&A answers can be a lead for your own question to the maker. They aren't a guarantee.

Brand-by-brand die notes

The matrix gives you the short version. Here's the detail behind each row, with the maker's own wording where it matters.

JD2

JD2's FAQ is unusually clear. Die sets are only interchangeable for Model 3 dies in a Model 4. Model 4 dies won't work in a Model 3, and no other current models are interchangeable. The FAQ also notes that the older Model 1 uses Model 4 dies, and the Model 2 uses Model 3 dies. JD2's Model 4 manual adds a practical note: older die sets often have 3 to 5 holes drilled through them. For the Model 32, buy Model 32 dies. A retailer, Trick-Tools, also notes that Model 32 dies over 2" OD are recommended for hydraulic benders only.

Rogue Fab

Rogue says all its dies are shared across the M601, M605 and M625, and that a die bought for an M601 works on an M625 without changes. Two catches. First, pin size: 2023-and-later benders use a 7/8" main pin, and older ones may use 3/4". Second, Rogue's FAQ says pressure die capacity should match the bender model. Moving from an M605 to an M625 needs a frame swap, but Rogue says the dies, hydraulics and cart carry over. See Rogue Fab M605 vs M625.

Baileigh

The RDB-050 has its own tooling. Baileigh's RDB-050 tooling page lists 90° and 180° sets from 3/4" to 2-1/2" round tube. Each set includes a forming die, counter die and hook sleeve. Baileigh's separate drop-on die sets fit its rotary-draw benders that accept drop-on tooling, but they're marked "Not RDB-050". For example, the 1-3/4" × 6" CLR drop-on set (DS-1750T-R600) isn't for the RDB-050. The RDB-050 versions carry their own part numbers, such as DS-90-1750T-R600 and DS-180-1750T-R600.

Pro-Tools

Pro-Tools names its 105 dies for the 105 Standard Duty and Heavy Duty benders, in 90° and 180° versions. The Brute and the Portabender have their own die pages. A retailer also describes Pro-Tools 105 dies as fitting the MB-105 and MB-105HD. If you own a different Pro-Tools model, check the die title before you buy.

Woodward Fab

Woodward Fab says its die sets are compatible with its WFB2 manual bender and its WFPB1000 hydraulic bender. It lists 180° round tube dies, 180° Schedule 40 pipe dies, a 120° round tube family and 120° square dies. Read each die's listing for its own capacity. More in our Woodward Fab WFB2 research review.

Eastwood

Eastwood's High Capacity dies come in 120° and 240° versions, sold separately, for round tube only. Its Amazon die listings are labelled for the High Capacity bender. Eastwood's separate Pro Former bender comes with its own dies, and we found no statement that they swap with the High Capacity.

Harbor Freight 12-ton

This ram bender ships with six cast dies for 1/2" to 2" Schedule 40 and 80 pipe. They're pipe shoes, not rotary-draw dies, so they don't belong in a tube bender comparison of die fit at all.

What has to match: the parts of a die set

When you ask "will this die fit?", you're really asking about several parts at once. Knowing their names helps you ask the maker a clear question, and it helps you spot a mismatch on a used die.

PartWhat it doesWhat has to match
Forming die (bend die)The grooved wheel the tube wraps aroundPin hole, height, drive features, tube OD and CLR
Clamp or hook sleeveGrips the tube to the die at the start of the bendThe forming die it pairs with, and the tube OD
Follower, counter or pressure dieSupports the outside of the tube as it bendsThe frame's mounting point and the tube OD
Main pinThe pivot the forming die turns onThe die's bore, such as 7/8" or 3/4" on Rogue Fab machines
Degree ring or stopShows or limits the bend angleThe die's start point and the frame

Two more details sit on the die itself. The CLR is fixed by the die and sets the bend radius. Our guide on centerline radius explains it. The bend angle family matters too. Many makers sell a short die (90° or 120°) and a long die (180° or 240°) in the same size. Eastwood, for example, recommends 90° as the max with its 120° dies, and 180° with its 240° dies.

💡Buy the set, not the wheel

A forming die without its matching clamp and follower is only half a die. When you buy used, make sure every piece of the set is there, and that all of them carry the same size and CLR.

Die pin size and machine versions

Pin size is the quickest way to rule a die in or out. The pin is the round shaft the forming die turns on. If the die's bore doesn't match the pin, the die won't seat properly. And the pin isn't always the same, even within one brand.

  • JD2: the Model 32 uses 1-1/4" alloy pins, and the Model 3 uses 7/8" pins, according to JD2.
  • Rogue Fab: benders made in 2023 and later use a 7/8" main pin. Earlier machines may use 3/4". Rogue labels its die bundles by pin size, sells press-in 3/4" bushings, and says you can measure the pin with calipers.
  • Rogue Fab conversion kits: Rogue sells kits that bring older M600-series machines up to its current designs, including a 7/8" pin version for the M625.
  • Other brands: we didn't find a published pin size for every model. Ask the maker.

How to measure your pin

Remove the forming die as your bender's manual describes. Measure the pin's diameter with calipers in two places, at right angles to each other. Write it down along with your machine's model and, if you can find it, its year or serial number. That one note answers half the questions a maker will ask you.

Machine versions matter for more than pins. Makers improve frames and change parts over the years. A die that fits a current model may not fit a ten-year-old one with the same name. When you email a maker, always give the model, the year if you know it, and the pin size you measured.

How to check die fit before you buy

Here's a simple routine that works for any brand. It takes ten minutes and an email. It can save you from a die that never fits.

  1. Identify your bender exactly. Model name, version, year if known, and drive type. Check the frame for a plate or serial number.
  2. Measure the pin. Use calipers on the main pin, as above.
  3. Measure your tube. Real OD and wall, with calipers. Remember pipe sizes don't match the real OD.
  4. Pick the die family. Decide on the CLR and the angle family you need, such as 120° or 240°.
  5. Find the maker's part number. Look it up on the maker's die page for your exact model.
  6. Read the die listing. Check the "fits" line, the wall basis or wall limits, and any notes like "Not RDB-050".
  7. Ask in writing if anything is unclear. Email the maker your model, pin size, tube and the die part number. Keep the reply.
  8. For used dies, ask for photos. Request clear shots of the bore, the clamp and the follower, plus any stamped part numbers.

If the maker says yes in writing, you're set. If the answer is "maybe" or "it should", treat it as a no until you've seen it on your machine. That sounds strict, but a die that half-fits is worse than no die at all.

Die ratings vs bender ratings

Compatibility isn't only about fit. A die that fits can still be the wrong die for your tube. That's because dies have their own ratings, and some makers publish them separately from the bender's headline capacity.

Examples from maker listings

  • Baileigh RDB-050: each die set notes the wall its CLR is based on. Baileigh lists .095" for the 1-3/4" × 6" set, .109" for 2" × 6", and .120" for 2-1/2" × 7".
  • Rogue Fab: its tube capacity chart gives a minimum and maximum wall for each die and material. For example, the 1.75" × 6.0" die lists .095" minimum and .250" maximum in carbon steel, with two pressure dies needed at .156" and up.
  • Woodward Fab: die listings carry their own max wall, such as .134" on the 2" × 6" CLR die.
  • Pro-Tools: minimum wall depends on the die, and Pro-Tools points you to its die charts.

So when you check a die, read two lines: "fits" and "capacity". A die that fits your bender but is rated for a thinner wall than your tube is still the wrong die. Our tube bender capacity chart gathers these die-level numbers in one place.

Used dies, bushings and "universal fit" claims

Used dies can be a smart buy. Dies don't wear out quickly when they're looked after. But a used die comes with no fit promise, so you have to do the checking yourself.

What to ask a used-die seller

  • Which bender, model and year was it used on?
  • Is the full set there: forming die, clamp and follower?
  • What's the bore size, and can you send a photo with calipers on it?
  • Are there part numbers stamped on the parts?
  • Any cracks, gouges or weld repairs?

Bushings and adapters

Some makers offer their own way to bridge versions. Rogue Fab, for example, sells press-in 3/4" bushings for older machines with smaller pins. That's a maker solution within its own range. We found no maker that sells an adapter to run another brand's dies, and we don't recommend machining dies yourself to make them fit.

"Fits most benders" and Q&A answers

You'll see owner answers in Q&A sections saying a die from one brand fits another. Some may be right. But an owner's answer isn't a maker's promise, and it may be about a different version. Treat it as a reason to email the maker, not as proof.

These are composite stories. The same situations come up again and again in garage-fab forums and build threads.

💬 The pin that didn't match

A builder buys a used die from the same brand as their bender. It's the right tube size and CLR. But their bender is an older version with a smaller pin, and the die is bored for the newer, larger pin. The fix turns out to be a maker-supplied bushing, but only after a week of back-and-forth.

Lesson: same brand doesn't always mean same pin. Measure first.

💬 The wrong die line

Someone orders a die set from their bender maker's website, in the right size. It arrives, and it's from the maker's drop-on line for its other benders. The listing said, in small print, that it wasn't for their model. Back it goes.

Lesson: read the "fits" line on every die listing, even from your own maker.

Common die compatibility mistakes (and fixes)

These mistakes cost builders the most money and time. Each one has a simple fix.

❌ Assuming same size means same fit. Two 1.75" × 6" dies from different brands can be totally different underneath.

✅ Fix: buy dies made for your exact bender, or get a written fit statement.

❌ Assuming every die from your brand fits. Baileigh's drop-on sets, for example, aren't for the RDB-050.

✅ Fix: read the "fits" line on every listing.

❌ Ignoring pin size and version. Rogue Fab changed to a 7/8" pin for 2023-and-later benders.

✅ Fix: measure your pin with calipers and note your machine's year.

❌ Buying the short die when you need hoops. A 120° die won't make a 180° bend.

✅ Fix: choose the long die family (180° or 240°) for hoops and U-bends.

❌ Forgetting the die's own wall rating. A die can be rated for less wall than the bender.

✅ Fix: check the die listing's wall note as well as the bender's chart.

❌ Trusting a Q&A answer as a fit guarantee. Owner answers aren't maker statements.

✅ Fix: email the maker with your model, pin size and the die part number.

Pro tips for building a die collection

Experienced builders treat dies as a long-term investment. These habits help your collection grow without waste.

  • 💡 Pick a house size. Many builders design most projects around one or two tube sizes, so one die set does most of the work.
  • 💡 Buy the long die first if you'll ever need hoops. A 180° or 240° die covers short bends too. A short die can't do hoops.
  • 💡 Label everything. Mark each set with size, CLR, angle and pin size. Keep the clamp and follower with their die.
  • 💡 Keep the paperwork. Save listings and maker emails that confirm fit. They matter when you resell or upgrade.
  • 💡 Think about resale. Dies for popular benders in common sizes are usually easier to sell on later.
  • 💡 Upgrade within a family when you can. Rogue Fab, for example, says dies carry over from an M605 to an M625.
🎯Your die list is part of the bender choice

Before you buy a bender, list the dies you'll need in the first year. Check that the maker sells all of them. A great frame with a thin die catalogue can leave you stuck.

Frequently asked questions

Are tube bender dies interchangeable between brands?

Not as a rule. None of the makers we checked state that their dies fit another brand's bender. Pin size, die height, clamps and followers all differ. Ask the maker before you buy.

Do JD2 Model 3 dies fit the Model 32?

JD2's FAQ says the only current interchange is Model 3 dies in a Model 4, and that no other current models are interchangeable. JD2 also says the Model 32 uses larger 1-1/4" pins. Buy Model 32 dies for a Model 32.

Will Rogue Fab M601 dies fit an M605 or M625?

Rogue Fab says its dies are shared across all three. Check that the die's pin size matches your machine, 7/8" or 3/4", and that your pressure die suits your model.

Can I use Baileigh drop-on dies on the RDB-050?

No. Baileigh marks its drop-on die sets "Not RDB-050". The RDB-050 uses its own 90° and 180° tooling sets.

What other benders take Woodward Fab WFB2 dies?

Woodward Fab says its dies fit the WFB2 and its WFPB1000 hydraulic bender. We found no maker statement about other brands, so that's unknown. Ask the maker.

What is die pin size?

It's the diameter of the main pin the forming die turns on. The die's bore must match it. Measure it with calipers. Rogue Fab, for example, uses 7/8" on 2023-and-later benders and 3/4" on some older ones.

Your die buying checklist

Run through this list before you order any die, new or used.

  • I know my bender's exact model, version and year
  • I've measured the main pin with calipers
  • I've measured my tube's real OD and wall
  • The die listing names my exact bender in its "fits" line
  • I've checked for exclusions, such as "Not RDB-050"
  • The die's wall rating or basis suits my tube
  • I've chosen the right angle family: short die or long die
  • The set includes the forming die, clamp and follower
  • For cross-brand or unclear fits, I have the maker's answer in writing
  • For used dies, I've seen photos of the bore and part numbers

That's the whole story on die fit. Buy from your bender's maker when you can, measure your pin, read the "fits" line, and ask in writing whenever the answer isn't clear. Your dies will last for years. ✅
