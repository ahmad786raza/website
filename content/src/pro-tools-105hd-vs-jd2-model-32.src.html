Two ratchet benders, both made for garage shops, both with long handles, 180° dies and a hydraulic upgrade. On paper they look like twins. So how do you choose in a Pro-Tools 105HD vs JD2 Model 32 match-up? The answer hides in a handful of numbers: tube size, wall thickness, material and how far you want to grow.

This guide lines the two benders up side by side using only what Pro-Tools and JD Squared publish. You'll see where they match, where they split, and which one fits the tube you actually plan to bend. We'll also explain the jargon as we go, so you can read any capacity chart with confidence.

Nobody at Benders Hub has bent tube on these two machines. This is a research comparison built from manufacturer pages and manuals, checked in October 2026. You can read how we research equipment. Both benders are sold direct by their makers, not on Amazon.

⚡ Quick answer

Both are manual rotary-draw benders that can go hydraulic later. The right one depends on your heaviest tube.

  • Pick the Pro-Tools 105HD if you bend 4130 chromoly by hand. Pro-Tools rates it to 2" × .134" 4130 with its extension handle.
  • Pick the JD2 Model 32 if you need thick wall or bigger tube. JD2 rates it to 2" × .250" DOM with hydraulics and lists round tube up to 2-1/2" OD.
  • For most mild-steel bumpers and racks up to 2" × .120", both are in range. Then dies, mounting and your upgrade plans decide it.

Pro-Tools 105HD vs JD2 Model 32 at a glance

Both machines are rotary-draw benders. The tube is clamped to a round die. As you work the ratchet handle, the die turns and draws the tube around it. A follower block supports the outside of the bend. It's the method behind most bumpers, sliders, racks and cages you'll see.

They have a lot in common. Each starts as a manual ratchet bender with a handle of about 36". Each sells 180° dies. Each has a hydraulic path built around a 10-ton cylinder. So the choice comes down to the edges of the capacity chart, not the basics.

Capacity: tube size, wall and pipe

A capacity rating is three numbers working together: outside diameter (OD), wall thickness and material. Change any one of them and the force needed changes too. That's why "bends 2-inch tube" tells you very little on its own. Our capacity chart guide walks through this in more depth.

Pro-Tools 105HD

Pro-Tools lists the 105HD for 1/2" to 2" OD round tube up to .134" wall. It also bends pipe from NPS 1/4 to 1-1/2, and square tube from 1/2" to 1-1/2". Pro-Tools gives a square-tube wall range of .065" to .125". Those are the published limits. We found no higher wall rating for the 105HD, even in its hydraulic package.

JD2 Model 32

JD2 lists the Model 32 for 1/2" to 2-1/2" OD round tube and pipe. By hand, JD2 says round tube over 2" × .120" wall needs hydraulics. The same goes for square tube over 1-1/2" and solid bar over 1". With hydraulics, JD2 rates the Model 32 to 2" OD × .250" wall DOM, square tube to 2" and solid bar to 1-1/2".

A quick word on pipe vs tube. Tube is sold by its real OD. Pipe is sold by a nominal size that doesn't match the outside. For example, 1-1/2" pipe measures 1.900" across, per the standard pipe tables. So a 1-1/2" tube die won't fit 1-1/2" pipe. Pro-Tools lists its pipe capacity by NPS, which helps. Whichever bender you buy, order dies for the material you'll really bend.

Bending 4130 chromoly by hand

4130 chromoly is a stronger steel alloy that's popular for race cars, buggies and light frames. It's also harder to bend than mild steel, and it springs back more. That's why a bender's mild-steel rating doesn't tell you what it can do in chromoly.

What Pro-Tools publishes

Pro-Tools says the 105HD can bend 2" × .134" 4130 by hand with its 36" extension handle. That's a clear, published chromoly number for a manual machine. It's the main reason the 105HD shows up in chromoly discussions. If you plan to bend 4130 by hand, that rating is worth a lot.

What JD2 publishes

We didn't find a separate 4130 wall rating for the Model 32 on JD2's pages. JD2's manual line for hand bending is round tube up to 2" × .120". Past that, it calls for hydraulics. JD2's manual does give one useful chromoly fact: 4130 springs back roughly twice as far as the same size and wall of welded mild steel. That's true no matter which bender you use.

⚠️A rating is a ceiling, not a target

Running any bender at its published limit, all day, is hard on the machine and on you. If your most common tube sits right at the edge of the chart, consider the machine with more headroom, or plan for hydraulics.

Here's what that springback note means in practice. If your mild-steel test bend came back a few degrees short, expect chromoly of the same size to come back about twice as short. Don't guess. Bend a scrap piece of the same batch, measure it and write it down. Our guide to benders for chromoly compares the published 4130 ratings across more machines.

A mild-steel rating tells you what a bender can do in mild steel. If you bend 4130, look for a 4130 number, and then test-bend anyway.

One more point: if chromoly is for a cage or roll bar, the welding method and tube spec come from your sanctioning body's rulebook. Benders Hub isn't a tech inspector or an engineer. Check the current rulebook and talk to your inspector before you buy tube.

Dies, CLR and bend angle

The bender frame is only half the tool. Every tube size and every bend radius needs its own die. Over time, your die collection will likely cost more than the frame. So it's worth understanding each maker's die system before you commit.

CLR, D of bend and wall factor in plain words

Centerline radius (CLR) is the bend radius measured to the middle of the tube. The die sets it. Pro-Tools explains two handy ratios on its wall factor blog. D of bend is CLR ÷ OD. Wall factor is OD ÷ wall. A lower D of bend means a tighter, harder bend. A higher wall factor means thinner wall and more risk of wrinkles. Pro-Tools puts the practical minimum CLR without a mandrel at about 3 × OD.

Here's a quick example. A 2" × .134" tube has a wall factor of about 14.9. On a 6" CLR die it bends at 3D. Our bend calculator works these out for any size.

Pro-Tools 105HD dies

Pro-Tools lists the 105 series with a minimum CLR of about 3 × OD and a maximum CLR of 7". Its dies bend to 180°, and some die pages describe that as 180° after springback. Check each die listing for its own limits.

JD2 Model 32 dies

JD2 sells Model 32 dies in 180° versions, listed by OD and CLR. Each die listing gives a recommended wall range. For example, JD2's 1" round die page lists 0.058" to solid. JD2's FAQ says Model 32 dies don't fit its other current models.

Do Pro-Tools dies fit the JD2, or the other way round? Neither maker says so. Don't assume it. Our die compatibility guide explains why cross-brand fit is so risky.

Going hydraulic later

Both benders let you start by hand and add hydraulics when your projects grow. That's a smart way to spread the cost. But the details differ, and they matter for your garage.

Pro-Tools 105HD hydraulic

Pro-Tools sells a hydraulic 105HD package built around a 10-ton cylinder. Hydraulics take the strain off your arms. Keep in mind that the 105HD's published wall limit is still .134". For bigger tube, Pro-Tools points to its larger 105 Brute, a 15-ton hydraulic bender listed for 2-1/2" OD and NPS 2 pipe.

JD2 Model 32 hydraulic

JD2 sells a hydraulic adapter kit that swaps the ratchet and handle for a cylinder mount. It also sells a hydraulic Model 32, listed both with and without a cylinder. JD2 recommends a 10-ton cylinder with a 14" stroke, and the pump is always extra. JD2's air/hydraulic pump calls for a compressor rated at 20 cfm and 110 to 175 psi. Its electric pump is 1-1/2 HP on single-phase 110 V. JD2's manual says never to run the bender above 4,000 psi.

Which one should you buy?

Skip "which is best" for a moment. Ask which one fits your tube, your growth plans and your garage. Here's how we'd think it through, using the published specs.

Choose the Pro-Tools 105HD if…

  • You bend 4130 chromoly by hand and want a published rating to lean on.
  • Your tube stays at 2" OD and .134" wall or lighter.
  • You bend pipe as well as tube, and like a capacity chart listed by NPS.
  • You want the option of a hydraulic package without changing machines.

Choose the JD2 Model 32 if…

  • You expect to bend .188" or .250" DOM one day. JD2 rates the Model 32 to 2" × .250" with hydraulics.
  • You need round tube bigger than 2" OD. The Model 32 is listed to 2-1/2".
  • You mostly bend mild steel at 2" × .120" or lighter by hand for now.
  • You like JD2's clear manual-to-hydraulic lines and its optional pedestal.

If you're torn

Look at your next three projects, not just the next one. If any of them needs thick DOM or tube over 2", the Model 32 has the published headroom. If they're all chromoly at 2" or under and you'd rather not buy hydraulics, the 105HD's manual rating fits better. For mild-steel bumpers and racks at common sizes, either will do the job. Then die availability in your size and CLR becomes the tiebreaker.

💡Price your first die before the frame

Look up the die for your most common tube on each maker's site. Check the CLR options and the recommended wall range. That one listing often settles the whole decision.

Real-life lessons from builders

The stories below are composites. The same situations come up again and again in garage-fab forums and build threads. They're not one person's story, but they're very common.

💬 The chromoly kart frame

A builder plans a light chromoly frame in tube under 2" and wants to bend by hand. They compare published 4130 ratings and choose the 105HD for its chromoly number. Their first bends still come back short, because they'd dialled in springback on mild steel.

Lesson: a published 4130 rating tells you the bender can do it. Only a test bend tells you how far to over-bend.

💬 The bull bar that outgrew the bender

Someone buys a bender for 1.75" tube projects. A year later, a front bar design calls for 2-1/2" round tube. Their bender tops out at 2". They end up buying a second machine.

Lesson: check the OD range against the biggest part you can imagine building, not just the first one.

💬 The pipe that wouldn't fit

A builder buys a 1-1/2" die and picks up "1-1/2 inch" material from a local yard. It won't go in. The yard sold them 1-1/2" pipe, which measures 1.900" across the outside.

Lesson: buy dies by real OD for tube and by NPS for pipe, and measure before you order.

Common mistakes when choosing

Most regrets with these two benders come from a short list of mistakes. Here they are, with a fix for each.

❌ Assuming they're the same because both "go to 2 inches." Their wall, material and OD limits differ.

✅ Fix: compare OD, wall and material together, row by row, against your own tube list.

❌ Reading a mild-steel rating as a chromoly rating. 4130 is harder to bend and springs back more.

✅ Fix: look for a published 4130 number. If there isn't one, ask the maker.

❌ Planning thick DOM on a .134" machine. Hydraulics don't raise a published wall limit on their own.

✅ Fix: if .188" or .250" wall is in your future, choose a bender rated for it now.

❌ Mixing up pipe and tube dies. Nominal pipe sizes don't match the real OD.

✅ Fix: measure with calipers and buy dies for the exact material you'll bend.

❌ Assuming dies swap between brands. Neither maker says they do.

✅ Fix: treat your first die as a commitment to that maker's die system.

❌ Forgetting the pump in the hydraulic plan. JD2's air pump wants a 20 cfm compressor.

✅ Fix: list the cylinder, pump and power supply you'll need before you choose an upgrade path.

Pro tips for either bender

These habits come from the makers' manuals and blogs, plus the common wisdom in build threads. They cost little and save a lot of tube.

  • 💡 Log the batch. Pro-Tools suggests noting the heat or batch number from the mill cert, because springback varies from batch to batch.
  • 💡 Zero the degree plate every time. JD2's manual says to rotate the plate until the pointer reads 0° and hand-tighten the nut before each bend.
  • 💡 Use the bend-length rule. JD2 writes it as CLR × degrees × .0175. A 90° bend on a 6-1/2" CLR die uses about 10-1/4" of tube.
  • 💡 Leave clamp room. Plan bends so there's always enough straight tube for the clamp block, or leave extra and trim it later.
  • 💡 Respect pressure limits. JD2's manual says never to exceed 4,000 psi. Match the pump to the maker's spec.
  • 💡 Check D of bend before you buy a die. Below about 3D, a non-mandrel bend gets risky, per Pro-Tools.
🎯Expert habit: one house size

Many experienced builders design most projects around one tube size, such as 1.75". One die set, one springback number to learn, and parts that look like they belong together.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Pro-Tools 105HD or the JD2 Model 32 better for chromoly?

For bending 4130 by hand, the 105HD has the published rating: 2" × .134" 4130 with its extension handle. We didn't find a separate 4130 rating for the Model 32. Either way, expect about twice the springback of mild steel.

Can the Pro-Tools 105HD bend 2" × .250" wall?

Not according to its published specs. Pro-Tools lists round tube to .134" wall. JD2 rates the Model 32 to 2" × .250" DOM with hydraulics.

Do Pro-Tools dies fit the JD2 Model 32?

Neither maker says they do, so don't plan on it. JD2 says Model 32 dies don't even fit its other current models. Buy dies made for the bender you own.

Can both benders bend pipe?

Pro-Tools lists the 105HD for NPS 1/4 to 1-1/2 pipe. JD2 lists the Model 32 for round tube and pipe from 1/2" to 2-1/2" OD. Remember pipe is sized by NPS, so match the die to the pipe, not the tube size.

Do I need hydraulics on either bender?

Not for common mild-steel work up to 2" × .120". JD2 says the Model 32 needs hydraulics above that. Hydraulics on either machine also make long sessions easier on your body.

How far can each bender bend?

Both work with 180° dies. Pro-Tools lists 180° for the 105HD, depending on the die. JD2 sells 180° die sets for the Model 32.
