How this tube bender comparison works

Every row in this comparison comes from a published source. That means the maker's product page, the maker's manual, or a retail listing for that exact machine. We checked them in October 2026. Specs change, so treat this as a snapshot and click through before you buy.

We followed three simple rules. First, if a number isn't published, the cell says "Not published". We don't fill gaps with forum guesses. Second, if two sources disagree, we show both and mark the cell with ⚠️. Third, we never mix test results into a spec sheet. Nobody at Benders Hub has bent tube on these seven machines, so there are no "real-world" scores here. You can read more about how we research equipment.

Why does this matter? Because tube bender listings are hard to compare. One maker rates its machine in DOM wall thickness. Another adds a chromoly figure. A third rates it in Schedule 40 pipe, which is a different sizing system. Put them in one table without care and you'd be comparing apples with hydraulic jacks.

What we compared

  • Type and drive: rotary draw or ram, and whether it's a lever, a ratchet or hydraulic.
  • Round tube range: the outside diameter (OD) the maker lists.
  • Max wall: the thickest mild steel or DOM wall the maker publishes.
  • 4130 chromoly: only when the maker gives a separate figure.
  • Max bend angle: including how the dies change it.
  • Where it's sold: Amazon, a store, or direct from the maker.

How to read this table

Start with the max wall column. It's the one that rules machines out fastest. "Bends 2-inch tube" means very little on its own, because 2" × .065" wall is easy and 2" × .120" wall takes far more force. Then check the 4130 column if you plan to bend chromoly. Makers usually rate it lower than mild steel, and two of the seven don't publish a separate figure at all.

Next, look at max bend. A roll bar main hoop or a U-shaped bed bar needs well over 90°. Finally, check where each machine is sold. Three are on Amazon. Three are sold direct by the maker. One lives in Harbor Freight stores.

Why the numbers drift

Most conflicts have a boring cause. A maker beefs up a frame and updates one page but not another. A retailer copies an older description. Or a spec table and the marketing copy were written by different people. None of that means anyone is lying. It does mean you should confirm the number that matters to your project.

The simplest fix is an email. Give the maker your exact tube: OD, wall, material and the die you plan to use. Ask, "Is this within the rated capacity of my machine?" A clear yes in writing is worth more than any spec table, including ours.

When two official numbers disagree, the right answer isn't the bigger one. It's the one the maker confirms for your exact tube.

The three benders sold direct from the maker

Three of the most talked-about garage benders aren't on Amazon. You buy them from the maker or its dealers. They have deep die catalogues and long track records in build threads. Here's what each maker publishes.

JD2 Model 32

The Model 32 is a horizontal rotary-draw bender for 1/2" to 2-1/2" OD round tube and pipe. JD2 describes it as a heavier-duty version of its Model 3, with larger 1-1/4" alloy pins. By hand, it uses a ratchet lever with an extension, about 36" in total. JD2 says round tube over 2" × .120" needs its hydraulic add-on. With hydraulics, it's rated to 2" × .250" DOM (JD2).

The Model 32 manual suggests a 10-ton cylinder with a 14" stroke. Listings differ on whether the cylinder and pump come with the hydraulic kit, so check the package. JD2's page doesn't state a max bend angle, but JD2 sells 180° die sets. Who it suits: builders who want a manual start with a proven hydraulic path. Sold direct from JD2.

Rogue Fab M605

The M605 is a vertical rotary-draw bender driven by a hydraulic ram. You can power it with a hand pump, air-over-hydraulic, or a 1.5 or 2.0 HP electric pump. Rogue rates it to 2.00" OD. Its catalogue says .188" wall, while its FAQ says .250" DOM. Rogue lists about 94° per ram stroke, and the machine keeps going past 180° with re-strokes.

Rogue says its M601, M605 and M625 share dies. Moving from an M605 to an M625 needs a frame swap, but the dies, hydraulics and cart carry over. Who it suits: builders who want hydraulic power from day one and like a vertical layout. Sold direct from Rogue Fab.

Pro-Tools 105HD

The 105HD handles 1/2" to 2" OD round tube up to .134" wall, plus NPS 1/4 to 1-1/2 pipe. Pro-Tools rates it on mild steel Schedule 40 pipe and lists 180° bends, depending on the die. The package includes a stand, a degree plate and a 36" extension handle. Pro-Tools says that handle gives enough torque to bend 2" × .134" chromoly by hand (Pro-Tools).

Pro-Tools gives a minimum CLR of about 3 × OD and a max CLR of 7". A hydraulic package is also sold. Who it suits: builders who bend chromoly by hand and want a published 4130 number. Sold direct from Pro-Tools. Comparing these two closely? See Pro-Tools 105HD vs JD2 Model 32.

The Harbor Freight 12-ton: a different kind of tool

The Central Machinery 12-ton hydraulic pipe bender (item 32888) sells in Harbor Freight stores. It's a ram bender. A bottle jack pushes a cast shoe into the middle of the pipe while two supports hold the ends. Rotary-draw benders work differently. They clamp the tube to a round die and draw it around that die.

Harbor Freight rates it for 1/2" to 2" Schedule 40 and 80 pipe, with six cast dies, a 9-1/2" stroke and bends up to 90° (Harbor Freight). The key line is in its manual. It says the bender is not intended for thin-walled pipe such as conduit or exhaust pipe.

So why is it in a tube bender comparison? Because many first-time buyers compare it with the others on tonnage. Tonnage isn't the right yardstick. A 12-ton press can easily flatten a tube that a manual rotary-draw bender bends cleanly. The type of bend matters more than the force behind it.

Who it suits: people bending heavy-wall pipe for gates, railings, farm and shop jobs, where a slightly flattened bend is fine. Who it doesn't suit: anyone building bumpers, sliders, frames or roll cages from structural tube. Never use a ram bender for cage tube. 🛑Not for roll cages

A roll cage or roll bar must follow your sanctioning body's current rulebook, including the bend radius. Ram benders don't make the smooth, round, fixed-radius bends cage builders rely on. Use a rotary-draw bender and confirm with your rulebook and tech inspector. See our safety notes.

How to choose a drive

Manual or ratchet suits most mild steel up to about 1.75" with common walls. It's quieter, simpler and teaches you how tube behaves. Hydraulic makes sense for 2" tube, thick wall, chromoly and long cage builds. JD2's own line is a useful marker: it says round tube over 2" × .120" needs hydraulics on its Model 32.

Check your shop before you choose a powered option. An air-over-hydraulic kit needs steady shop air. An electric pump needs the right outlet. For a deeper look, read manual vs hydraulic tube benders.

Dies: the other half of every comparison

The bender frame is only half the purchase. Every tube size and every centerline radius (CLR) needs its own die set. On all six rotary-draw machines here, you buy dies separately. Over time, most builders spend more on dies than on the frame. So a fair tube bender comparison has to include the die side.

What the makers say about die fit

  • JD2: its FAQ says the only interchange between current models is Model 3 dies in a Model 4. No other current models are interchangeable (JD2 FAQ).
  • Rogue Fab: dies are shared across the M601, M605 and M625. Benders built from 2023 use a 7/8" main pin, and older ones may use 3/4", so check yours.
  • Baileigh: the RDB-050 uses its own die sets. Baileigh's drop-on sets for its other rotary-draw benders say "Not RDB-050".
  • Pro-Tools: dies are labelled for the 105 Standard Duty and Heavy Duty benders.
  • Woodward Fab: dies fit the WFB2 and the WFPB1000 hydraulic bender.
  • Eastwood: High Capacity dies come in 120° and 240° versions, for round tube only.

No maker in this group says its dies fit another brand's bender. So for cross-brand fit, the honest answer is unknown, ask the maker. Our full guide to tube bender die compatibility goes deeper, and our compatibility tool helps you plan a setup.

Common tube bender comparison mistakes (and fixes)

Most comparison regrets come from reading the specs too quickly. Here are the mistakes we see most often in forum threads, with a simple fix for each.

❌ Comparing tonnage across bender types. A 12-ton ram and a manual rotary-draw bender aren't on the same scale.

✅ Fix: compare the type first, then the published OD, wall and material ratings.

❌ Reading only the OD. "2-inch capacity" says nothing about wall or material.

✅ Fix: match OD, wall and material together, using the maker's own wording.

❌ Picking the bigger number when specs conflict. The bigger figure may be from an older page.

✅ Fix: plan around the lower figure, and ask the maker to confirm the higher one in writing.

❌ Assuming mild steel ratings cover 4130. Several makers rate chromoly lower.

✅ Fix: look for a separate 4130 figure. If there isn't one, ask before you buy.

❌ Ignoring the die's own limits. A die can be rated for less wall than the machine.

✅ Fix: read each die listing's wall and CLR notes, not just the bender's headline.

❌ Assuming dies swap between brands. No maker here says they do.

✅ Fix: budget for dies from the same maker as your bender, unless a maker or retailer states otherwise.

Pro tips for comparing benders yourself

Specs change, and new machines appear. These habits let you run your own comparison any time, with any brand.

  • 💡 Write down your hardest tube first. OD, wall and material. That one line rules out more machines than anything else.
  • 💡 Save the source page. Take a screenshot or PDF of the spec page on the day you buy. If a rating changes later, you'll know what you were sold.
  • 💡 Price one die with every bender. Compare "frame plus your first die", not frame alone.
  • 💡 Check the die family's angle. Many makers sell 90° or 120° dies and separate 180°+ dies. The bender's max angle only matters if you buy the long die.
  • 💡 Look for a published 4130 figure. If you'll ever bend chromoly, a machine with a stated rating saves you a lot of guessing.
  • 💡 Ask about pins and versions. Rogue Fab, for example, says benders built from 2023 use a 7/8" main pin, while older ones may use 3/4". Older machines can need different dies or bushings.
  • 💡 Run the geometry. Our bend calculator shows D of bend and wall factor for your tube and die, so you can spot tough combinations early.
🎯The one-email test

Before you buy, email the maker your tube size, wall, material and planned die. Ask if it's within the rated capacity. How fast and clearly they answer tells you a lot about the support you'll get later.

Frequently asked questions

Which tube bender is best in this comparison?

It depends on your hardest tube. On published specs, the Baileigh RDB-050 has the biggest mild steel OD of the Amazon machines, the JD2 Model 32 has a 2" × .250" DOM rating with hydraulics, and the Pro-Tools 105HD has the clearest manual chromoly rating. Start with the table and filter by your tube.

Which tube bender can bend 2" × .250" DOM?

JD2 rates the Model 32 to 2" × .250" DOM with hydraulics. Rogue Fab's FAQ gives the M605 .250" DOM, but its catalogue says .188", so ask Rogue Fab. Rogue rates its M625 to 2.00" × .250" DOM.

Can the Harbor Freight 12-ton bend tube for a roll cage?

No. It's a ram pipe bender rated for Schedule 40 and 80 pipe, and its manual says it isn't meant for thin-wall pipe. Cage tube needs a rotary-draw bender and a die that meets your rulebook.

Are dies interchangeable between these benders?

Not as far as any maker states. Some dies swap within a brand, such as Rogue Fab's M601, M605 and M625. For cross-brand fit, ask the maker before you buy.

Which of these benders can make 180° bends?

Eastwood with its 240° dies, Pro-Tools 105HD, Baileigh RDB-050 and Rogue Fab M605 all list 180° or more. JD2 and Woodward Fab sell 180° die sets. The Harbor Freight ram bender goes up to 90°.

Is the JD2 Model 32 still made?

JD2's product page is still up, but at least one retailer lists the Model 32 as discontinued. Contact JD2 for current status before you plan around it.
